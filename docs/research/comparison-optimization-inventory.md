## Scope challenges and limits

- The inventory's “optimization methods” family spans different problem classes: LP feasibility, hull redundancy, convex terminal minimization, Euclidean projection, and convex-cost maximization. One general optimizer comparison would not be apples-to-apples; each requires matching objective/constraints and independent oracle evidence. Sources: [algorithm-module-inventory.md:18–39, 88–121](algorithm-module-inventory.md), [OPT-01](../superpowers/plans/tickets/OPT-01.md).
- Zoutendijk is supported by both paper mentions and a concrete Pascal `ZODENTEK`, but the implementation evidence shows projection rather than a general-purpose nonlinear optimization solver. [algorithm-module-inventory.md:88–110](algorithm-module-inventory.md)
- The archive does not establish a literal recursive split-and-merge hull algorithm; OPT-11 is an extension. Nor does A16 establish cross-query warm starts; OPT-12 is separate. [algorithm-module-inventory.md:51, 63–80](algorithm-module-inventory.md)
- `CHEBR`'s name is not enough to claim Chebyshev-center optimization; the existing source audit describes an enclosing Euclidean ball over finite points and says derivation is needed. [algorithm-module-inventory.md:121](algorithm-module-inventory.md)
- B02–B09 entries are reported article results with materially different models/revisions and recovery limits; no claim here treats them as independently reproduced. [baselines.md](baselines.md)
