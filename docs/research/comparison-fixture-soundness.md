# Scientific soundness of the proposed comparison fixture ladder

Date: 2026-10-17. This is a research assessment of the fixture/resource/oracle proposal in the [owner-review draft PR #410](https://github.com/butahlecoq/controlled-systems-lab/pull/410), not an approval or implementation decision. Fixture choices in live issue [#399](https://github.com/butahlecoq/controlled-systems-lab/issues/399) are recorded as owner decisions; resource/oracle details in the draft remain subject to its review gate. No benchmark or legacy program was run for this note.

## Findings in brief

The proposed dimensions `{2,3,4,8,16}`, sizes `{250,1000,5000}`, deterministic unscrambled Halton points in `[0,1]^d`, plus explicit simplex/interior and rank-deficient duplicate/boundary cases, make a reproducible *synthetic* suite. They do not sample the same distributions as historical B03, and should not be described as a B03 reproduction or as representative of all random-cloud workloads. Hull reduction and H/V conversion must be reported separately: a finite point-cloud hull has at most `n` output vertices, but its facet representation can be combinatorially much larger. No repository evidence supports a runtime estimate at 5000×16. Exact checks on represented binary64 inputs can prove finite-cloud hull statements; numerical LP status, sampled support directions, and agreement between the compared implementations cannot alone prove completeness. Wall time is enforceable by a supervising process; a portable 8-GiB resident-memory *cap* is not guaranteed. Preserve every partial/refused/limited result and identify enforcement vs observation.

## 1. Distributional scope and bias

The B03 source summary reports 5000-point hull construction from Normal, Uniform, and Sphere sampling in dimensions 2–5, but says the full arrays and seeds are absent ([`docs/research/baselines.md`](baselines.md), B03 entry; static source inventory in [`comparison-hull-vhv-coverage.md`](comparison-hull-vhv-coverage.md), §1). The primary historical evidence therefore establishes only those distribution names and dimensions, not exact parameterization, normalization, seeds, or data. The new proposal correctly labels its clouds synthetic rather than recovered B03 inputs ([PR #410 draft](https://github.com/butahlecoq/controlled-systems-lab/pull/410), fixture paragraph; issue [#399](https://github.com/butahlecoq/controlled-systems-lab/issues/399)).

Halton is a deterministic low-discrepancy sequence, not an IID draw from any of those three distributions. Mapping its coordinates directly into the unit box tests an axis-aligned bounded domain and tends to fill it more evenly than random samples. This is useful for repeatability and broad coverage, but it omits stochastic variability, tail behavior, concentration near a center or shell, and sensitivity to a particular historical generator. It also introduces sequence-order and coordinate-axis structure; scrambling disabled means those structures are not randomized away. Thus results may favor methods whose ordering/conditioning works well on evenly spread box data, and cannot establish expected performance on IID Normal/Uniform/Sphere populations. The foundational Halton sequence construction is described by Halton (1960), [“On the efficiency of certain quasi-random sequences of points in evaluating multi-dimensional integrals,” Numerische Mathematik 2, 84–90](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01386213); its quasi-random integration purpose is not a guarantee of representative hull-complexity distributions.

Recommended disclosure: call this the **deterministic unit-box synthetic track**; publish generator/version, index convention, point ordering, coordinate mapping, content hash, and all statuses. Keep any future seeded IID Normal, uniform-volume-in-box, and normalized-sphere tracks separate, with fully stated parameterizations and multiple seeds. A normalized sphere is a surface distribution, unlike uniform volume in a box; neither should be conflated with the other. It is an owner choice whether to add one or more IID tracks now; they are not required to make the current reproducible baseline scientifically honest.

## 2. Output size and feasibility

For hull reduction, output vertices are a subset of the `n` input points, so output size is bounded by `n=5000` regardless of dimension. This does *not* bound the amount of intermediate LP work: the LP-membership reducer forms candidate-specific feasibility problems (contract [`docs/contracts/hull-reduction.md`](../contracts/hull-reduction.md), LP-membership profile), while basis-reuse and ordered strategies have different work profiles. Degeneracy, redundant-point fraction, numerical conditioning, and implementation/provider behavior matter. The simplex-plus-interior and duplicate/rank-deficient fixtures deliberately exercise easy redundancy and degeneracy; the unit-box low-discrepancy cloud typically emphasizes spread/extreme points. They do not span all these factors.

For H/V conversion, output facets can dominate. For a full-dimensional convex polytope with `n` vertices in dimension `d`, the Upper Bound Theorem gives the cyclic-polytope maximum number of facets; for even `d=2k`, this is `C(n-k,k)+C(n-k-1,k-1)` (odd dimensions have the corresponding two-binomial formula). See McMullen, [“The maximum numbers of faces of a convex polytope,” Mathematika 17 (1970), 179–184](https://doi.org/10.1112/S002557930001339X), and Ziegler, *Lectures on Polytopes*, Springer (1995), ch. 8. These are theoretical worst-case bounds, not predictions for these fixtures. Even the dominant term at `n=5000,d=16` is on the order of `C(4992,8)`, far beyond a materializable facet list; actual clouds can be far smaller, and no timing or memory observation for this ladder exists. Conversely, conversion may fail or refuse before reaching a complete output. Record generated-input size, affine rank, input/output counts, completion status, and resource outcomes separately.

The present Qhull adapter is explicitly bounded binary64 and can refuse nonempty rays, unbounded H inputs, ambiguous rank, and assurance profiles it cannot certify ([`docs/contracts/polyhedral-conversion.md`](../contracts/polyhedral-conversion.md), limits; [`src/adapters/polyhedral/qhull.cpp`](../../src/adapters/polyhedral/qhull.cpp)). Therefore the fixture ladder can establish neither general high-dimensional H/V feasibility nor behavior outside its bounded accepted cases. Keep hull reduction and V/H conversion as separate result families and make unsupported/incomplete cells visible. A report must not infer “fast at dimension 16” from hull reduction when facet enumeration or conversion was not completed.

## 3. Independent correctness evidence

### Finite input hull reduction

For each returned set of source indices, check uniqueness and range. Then prove both inclusions for the exact finite-cloud hull:

1. **Returned points are from the input** by the index check, hence their convex hull is contained in `conv(P)`.
2. **Every input point lies in the returned hull**, which proves `conv(P)` is contained in `conv(Q)`.

With exact arithmetic for the stored binary64 values, point-in-hull is a rational feasibility problem: each binary64 coordinate is an exact dyadic rational, and one can check `Q λ = p`, `1ᵀλ=1`, `λ≥0`. A proof-producing exact rational solver or independently checkable primal/dual certificates gives a complete check. Repository hull contracts distinguish exact certification of represented binary64 candidates from claims about pre-rounding real-valued data ([`docs/contracts/hull-reduction.md`](../contracts/hull-reduction.md); [`docs/research/geo02-floating-model-audit.md`](geo02-floating-model-audit.md)). Do not silently call a floating-point LP “exact” merely because its residual is small.

The draft's corrected condition—check every input point in each returned hull *and* mutual old/new containment—is sounder than comparing only the two outputs ([#399 live comments](https://github.com/butahlecoq/controlled-systems-lab/issues/399); [PR #410](https://github.com/butahlecoq/controlled-systems-lab/pull/410)). But an oracle built by calling either compared reducer, or by converting one reducer's output to an oracle representation, is circular. Pin an independent exact checker, and test it against analytic simplex/box/segment/singleton fixtures and the existing rational geometry edge-case tests at base commit `d5a48bb`.

If only floating LP is available, report solver, tolerances, primal residuals, dual status, and that evidence is numerical rather than a complete proof. Sampled support directions can reveal discrepancies but are not a completeness proof: an omitted vertex can be exposed only in a direction not sampled. Likewise, mutual output containment alone permits both methods to omit the same input vertices.

### Bounded V/H conversion

For a V-representation to H-representation, verify every emitted inequality is valid on all input generators, then prove the reverse inclusion (the H-system contains no points outside the input convex hull). For H-to-V, verify all returned vertices satisfy all input inequalities, then prove the returned convex hull covers the entire bounded feasible set. These are two different directions; checking only residuals at returned points proves validity but not completeness. Exact rational LP implication/containment checks or independently checked dual certificates can establish them for exact rationalized input. For the represented binary64 contract, explicitly define whether values are interpreted as exact dyadics or as rounded numerical data; the conversion adapter's floating residual assurance is not itself a proof of global coverage ([`docs/research/geo02-floating-model-audit.md`](geo02-floating-model-audit.md), H-path coverage discussion; [`docs/contracts/polyhedral-conversion.md`](../contracts/polyhedral-conversion.md)).

If a full exact proof is not affordable at high dimensions, keep sampled support discrepancies and numerical bidirectional LP checks as *diagnostics*, mark completeness unproved, and do not promote them to “round-trip correctness.” Also avoid using the compared converter or same Qhull backend as the only oracle. Oracle independence should include distinct implementation/dependency where practical, plus analytic fixtures.

## 4. Resource-budget semantics

The proposal's 300-second wall-clock budget is enforceable if a parent supervisor can terminate the child process/process group and records timeout separately from solver failure. The 8-GiB “resident memory” cap is platform-dependent: Linux cgroups/resource limits can enforce memory accounting, while Windows job objects can impose process/job memory limits, but neither automatically measures the same scope or has uniform availability/configuration. macOS and constrained CI/container environments may have different facilities. The POSIX `getrusage` interface reports resource use but is not a cap ([Linux kernel cgroup v2 memory controller documentation](https://docs.kernel.org/admin-guide/cgroup-v2.html#memory); [Microsoft Job Objects documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/job-objects); [getrusage(2)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/getrusage.2.html)).

Record requested limit; whether enforced; mechanism and scope (single process vs descendants/job/container); observed peak and source; elapsed time; termination reason; and whether output is complete. `observed peak memory` must never be represented as “enforced cap.” If enforcement is unavailable, call the attempt observation-only or omit it from the capped profile. A process killed at a limit is a resource-limited attempt, not a geometric failure or absent row. Preserve partial outputs only as incomplete, never as successful complete conversions. The PR draft already asks to separate requested, enforced, observed, and termination metadata; ensure its 300s/8-GiB wording follows these platform distinctions.

## 5. Compact evidence matrix and remaining choices

| Track | Dimensions / size | Purpose and evidence | Main limitation / tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deterministic box cloud | `d={2,3,4,8,16}`, `n={250,1000,5000}` | Reproducible scaling track; Halton start 1, no scramble; exact input hash | Synthetic low-discrepancy box coverage, not historical or IID distribution |
| Simplex with interior points | Selected small/medium `n` in each shared dimension | Known hull with controllable redundancy; easy analytic/exact oracle | Favorable to redundancy-detection strategies; specify interior construction and ratios |
| Rank-deficient/duplicate/boundary | At least one case per dimension, including embedded low-rank geometry | Degeneracy, duplicates, boundary and rank refusal behavior | Keep separate from full-dimensional timing summaries; clarify intended rank profile |
| Historical B03 track | Only if arrays/seeds/generator are recovered | Actual historical comparison at dimensions 2–5 | Currently unreproducible; not substituted by synthetic fixtures |
| Optional IID distribution tracks | If owner elects: named box-uniform, Gaussian, sphere surface; multiple pinned seeds | Measures stochastic/distribution sensitivity | More cases and seeds increase cost; parameters must be sourced or explicitly new |

Across the table, report hull reduction separately from H/V conversion and retain all 300-second/resource-limited/refused/incomplete outcomes. Candidate questions left for owner: whether to add IID tracks now; how many structural variants per dimension; whether H/V conversion is limited to small tractable `n` separate from the hull size ladder; which platform/resource mechanism defines a comparable 8-GiB cap; and what level of exact high-dimensional proof is mandatory versus a clearly labeled numerical/incomplete result. This note makes no choice on those questions.

## Evidence limitations in prior audit

Preserve prior research dossiers as historical records. Two specific limitations affect their use: commit `c17d5fe8b59e54372f88837560ff51c98a4e514c` (`comparison-hull-vhv-coverage.md`) has misspelled GitHub owner links in cited URLs, so those links should not be treated as reliable navigation; and its §2.2 heading/§7 assertion that all measured reports are single-adapter is contradicted by its own OPT-06 row, which explicitly describes basis-reuse **vs LP baseline** paired measurements. These are narrow evidence-description errors, not a reason to rewrite or discard that historical audit. Its static-source caveat and other inventories remain useful subject to checking the cited sources.
